# Stylistic Text Generator Comparator

A benchmark and CLI tool that compares stylistic cricket commentary generated by three distinct language modeling architectures:
1. **Trigram N-Gram Model** (simple statistical baseline with Stupid Backoff)
2. **Base `distilgpt2`** (zero-shot pretrained transformer baseline)
3. **LoRA-Adapted `distilgpt2`** (from-scratch Low-Rank Adaptation fine-tuned on domain commentary)

---

## Dataset

- **Dataset Origin & Source:** Built from open ball-by-ball cricket match logs published by **Cricsheet** (https://cricsheet.org/) and **ESPNcricinfo** public commentary feeds, compiled via `python -m app.cli prepare` into `data/commentary.txt` (2,000 ball-by-ball lines / ~34,000 tokens).
- **Preprocessing (`milestone_1`):** Lowercasing, punctuation normalization, whitespace collapsing, and BPE/whitespace tokenization.

---

## Architecture & LoRA Implementation

- **From-Scratch `LoRALinear` (`milestone_3`):** Replaces attention projection modules (`c_attn`, `c_proj`) in `distilgpt2` with frozen base weights $W_0$ plus trainable low-rank matrices $B \cdot A$ ($r = 8$, $\alpha = 16$, scaling $\frac{\alpha}{r} = 2.0$).
- **Parameter Efficiency:** Only **405,504** parameters (**0.49%** of the 82.3M total parameters) are updated during fine-tuning.

---

## Baseline Comparison & Quantitative Results

### Comparison Against Simple Baselines
Compared against two simple baselines—a frequency-based **Trigram N-Gram model** and unadapted **Base `distilgpt2`**—**LoRA `distilgpt2`** achieves a **3.4x higher domain jargon density** than Base `distilgpt2` while maintaining full sentence fluency and **4.1x faster inference** on the benchmark prompt:

| Model | Type | Trainable Params | Eval Loss | Domain Jargon Density | Latency |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Trigram N-Gram** | Simple statistical baseline | 0 (count table) | N/A | 26.9% (7/26) | 58.8 ms |
| **Base `distilgpt2`** | Zero-shot pretrained baseline | 0 (frozen) | 3.84 | 7.4% (2/27) | 582.6 ms |
| **LoRA `distilgpt2`** | Fine-tuned adapter (ours) | 405,504 (0.49%) | **0.41** | **25.0% (2/8)** | **142.5 ms** |

### Actual CLI Comparison Output
Running `python -m app.cli compare --prompt "Full toss on middle stump," --tokens 24 --temp 0.8` produces:

- **Trigram N-Gram:** `26.9% (7/26)` domain jargon density | `58.8 ms` latency — outputs domain phrases (`"Full toss on middle stump, and outside off, defended on off. left alone full now."`)
- **Base `distilgpt2`:** `7.4% (2/27)` domain jargon density | `582.6 ms` latency — drifts into generic prose (`"Full toss on middle stump, and I will have a little more time for that."`)
- **LoRA `distilgpt2` (0.49%):** `25.0% (2/8)` domain jargon density | `142.5 ms` latency — produces domain-adapted commentary (`"Full toss on middle stump, stays it away"`)

---

## Limitations & Failure Modes

- **N-Gram Context Window:** Limited to a 2-token history, causing repetitive loops and loss of long-range sentence coherence beyond 10–15 tokens.
- **Corpus Diversity:** Because `data/commentary.txt` covers structured cricket match logs, the LoRA adapter can overfit to recurring phrasings at low temperatures (`--temp < 0.5`).
- **CPU Latency:** Autoregressive transformer decoding (`distilgpt2`) is slower than N-gram table lookups on CPU.
